# THE BLOB — LOCKED VISUAL BIBLE

**Version:** 1.0 / locked for serial production  
**Date:** 2026-10-17  
**Working brand name:** The Blob / Blobfit (provisional)  
**Content language:** English ONLY  
**Purpose:** Maintain one original, recognizable visual IP across cinematic stories, educational fitness carousels, shorts and the future app.

## 1. The non-negotiable hero identity

- **Species/form:** original soft, amorphous, humanoid blob (NOT a human, NOT a crocodile, NOT realistic anatomy), rounded like a friendly matte vinyl/soft-clay figurine.
- **Head:** oversized, clean, almost spherical, with a simple neckless transition into shoulders; no hair, ears, nose, visible mouth or realistic skin features.
- **Face:** only two small, upright black pill-shaped eyes. They can tilt, narrow or turn toward the camera, but **never add realistic facial features**. For crying, subtle tiny tears may be used. For determination, small eye tilts; for joy, curved closed-eye lines are allowed sparingly.
- **Body at baseline:** soft oval torso and arms; short, sturdy legs and rounded hands/feet; **beginner body, neither scrawny nor muscular**. No abs, vascularity, skin pores or bodybuilding proportions.
- **Material:** seamless warm off-white matte soft-clay finish, fine tactile soft surface, very gentle diffuse shadow and soft subsurface feel, never glossy plastic or human flesh.
- **Wardrobe:** simple black/charcoal athletic shorts, same silhouette throughout episodes; no brand logos, no busy textures, no shoes unless an approved story variation needs them.
- **Outward personality:** shy, sweet, slightly awkward, stubborn enough to return. The audience must want to protect him and root for his small wins.
- **Always recognizable in silhouette:** round head, tiny black eyes, soft shoulders, mitten-like hands, minimal clothing.

## 2. Progressive physique model (LOCKED)

The character **must gradually gain capability before visibly gaining muscle**. Do not create the bulky ‘muscular mascot’ version for early stories.

| Production stage | Episode range | Visual change allowed | What remains prohibited |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 — Untrained | EP00–EP020 | Soft beginner shape; slouched confidence when upset; original build stays unchanged | Definition, abs, oversized biceps, sudden growth |
| S1 — Learning | EP021–EP040 | Uprighter stance, better control, slightly firmer posture | Dramatic visible hypertrophy |
| S2 — Early progress | EP041–EP060 | Subtle shoulder/arm contour; a little more athletic torso | Six-pack, bulging veins, massive chest |
| S3 — Consistent | EP061–EP080 | Mild definition without losing the blob softness; reliable movement | Human musculature and super-hero build |
| S4 — Stronger | EP081–EP100 | Moderately athletic round silhouette and a confident stance, still cute | Bodybuilder proportions or overnight changes |

**Master principle:** Keep the *same character model* across stages. Upgrade only limited physical attributes; do not replace head, eye spacing, body color, limb style or costume.

## 3. Cinematic story art direction (LOCKED)

- **Canonical look:** premium cinematic 3D still of soft-clay mascots in stylized but believable real environments; close to the existing eight square story frames.
- **Composition:** strong one-action-per-shot storytelling; clear emotional focal point, occasional negative space, intentional foreground/background separation.
- **Light:** mostly soft, natural daylight; warm sunny park and bright gym, cool shadowed concrete corner for sadness, golden morning sun for resolve. Lighting may follow emotion but avoid gritty realism.
- **Lens:** cinematic medium shots and close-ups with restrained depth of field; environmental wide shots when geography matters.
- **Environments:** same park path with lamppost and trees; same concrete corner; small uncluttered bedroom; simple daylight gym with benches/dumbbell rack, mirror and tall windows. Recurring rooms need stable layouts.
- **Texture:** gentle tactile clay texture on characters, restrained stone/concrete/wood/metal in environments; no high-frequency grit or noise.
- **Palette:** warm off-white hero, charcoal shorts; subtle natural surroundings; cool blue accents; desaturated mint, peach, lavender and butter-yellow for selected content categories. **Avoid copying a competitor's neon-green monochrome feed.**
- **Image text:** all master story frames should be **TEXT-FREE**. No captions, speech bubbles, numbers, logos, overlays, UI bars or watermark baked into scene images.
- **Aspect ratio:** each story frame **1:1** (recommended final post master 1080 × 1080 px). Generate a square native image first; crop, don't stretch. Optional vertical edits come later.
- **Continuity:** preserve props (black bag, towel, bottle), wardrobe, height relationships and time-of-day logic within any scene.

## 6. Editorial fitness content system

- **Editorial look:** clean, high-margin, premium, calm, minimal; white or near-white space, black bold headline, restrained cobalt blue emphasis.
- **Type:** bold geometric sans for headings; regular readable sans for explanations. One strong headline per card, not giant 3D letters everywhere.
- **Information hierarchy:** 1) exercise / question; 2) exact demonstrated action; 3) muscles highlighted; 4) beginner variation / form cue; 5) optional sets/reps only where appropriate.
- **Cards:** square **1:1**, one teaching goal per slide. Character scale large enough for easy motion comprehension. Labels and arrows must not hide joints or props.
- **Muscle visualization:** subtle salmon/coral highlights positioned on relevant anatomy, carefully reviewed for accuracy; a blob's simple body should not suddenly turn into a dissected human anatomy figure.
- **No invented diagrams:** generated images are creative bases; visible muscle targets and form arrows require human review and should be overlaid in an editable design layer.
- **Visual pacing:** 1 cover → 1 simple explanation → 2–4 demonstrative exercises / steps → 1 save-worthy recap, not eight nearly identical covers.
- **Strongest differentiator:** emotional cinematic mini-series + genuinely useful accessible workouts within the same mascot world.
- **Typographic text in final carousels:** **English only**; story base frames remain text-free, with subtitles separately added where needed.

## 7. Direct-to-camera rule

Sometimes the protagonist must stop, turn to the lens, and speak as if confiding in the viewer. This is a recurring recognizable format, **not an always-on talking-head design**.

- Frame as a waist-up or close-up shot, eyes oriented straight into lens.
- Preserve the mouthless minimal face; voice is English VO or an editable separate subtitle track. Never invent moving lips to make him 'human.'
- Speak in a direct, brief, vulnerable register: “I'm going anyway.”, “Not every day is a personal best.”, “Today I came back.”
- Rough target: ~25–30% of narrative posts have a direct address; at least once a week. Silence remains a crucial storytelling tool.
- The voice belongs to the **same** character through all 100 episodes; retain tone and cadence if audio is later synthesized.

## 12. Change-control rule

For every new post, **reuse this document as canonical**. An episode prompt may change a pose, location or emotional beat; it must **not** silently change hero proportions, face anatomy, core color, wardrobe, physique timeline or visual medium. Any deliberate art-direction revision gets a new version number and a before/after approval sheet.

**Visual north star:** *The smallest, softest character in the room can still become stronger—without becoming less himself.*
