# THE BLOB — STORY AND CONTENT ROADMAP (EPISODES 00–100)

**Status:** Locked creative direction / production roadmap v1.0  
**Created:** 2026-10-17  
**Working brand name:** The Blob / Blobfit (NOT yet a finalized commercial name)  
**Audience:** Global, English-first  
**Platforms:** Instagram carousel + Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts; reusable later inside the fitness app.

## 1. Canon and episode numbering

- **Episode 00 — THE PROMISE** is the already-established eight-image origin story: the cream-white protagonist sees the pink character with a very muscular grey character, withdraws, cries, makes a promise to himself in a mirror, and attempts his first dumbbell curls. This is already Content #1, not a new assignment.
- **Episodes 01–100** are **100 NEW publishable pieces of content** over **50 days at two posts per day**. Thus there are **101 numbered episodes including the pre-existing Episode 00**. This resolves the requested “0–100” timeline without counting the origin twice.
- The next content to produce is **Episode 01 — The Morning After the Promise**, followed on the same day by **Episode 02 — Your First Gym Visit**.
- The daily rhythm is **A: narrative episode (odd number)** then **B: educational/practical fitness content (even number)**. The educational post pays off the exact problem, emotion, or gym experience established in the story post.
- **Two social posts per day are not two hard workouts per day.** Training, mobility, walking and recovery follow a sensible progression.

## 2. Series promise

**Logline:** After a painful moment in the park, a small, soft-bodied blob enters a gym to become “stronger.” The audience watches him discover that strength means skill, consistency, self-respect, connection and recovery—not becoming someone else.

**Emotional engine:** heartbreak → embarrassing first steps → tiny wins → comparison → patient training → setbacks → genuine confidence → helping another beginner.

**Why audiences return:** Did he go back today? Did he learn the movement? Is he getting stronger? Will he look at the camera and tell us how he really feels?

**Guardrail:** He must not “win back” the pink character or defeat the grey figure to prove his worth. The initial experience motivates him, but the long-term choice is for himself. The pink and grey characters are people in the story, not villains or targets for humiliation; no unsupported claim of cheating.

## 5. Publishing mechanics

**Story (A / odd EPs):** typically 6–8 square 1:1 still frames, or a 15–30 second motion edit made from them. Strong clear visual cause/effect; 1 emotional hook; one memorable turn; one unresolved question, payoff, or small win. **No baked-in text** in master story frames. Use separate editable English captions, music, SFX and (when called for) English voiceover.

**Utility (B / even EPs):** typically 5–7 square 1:1 cards. Clean editorial design, instructional poses, optional color-coded muscle targeting, safe movement descriptions, beginner options and one save-worthy actionable takeaway. English text can be added in a controllable design layer, not trusted to image generation.

**Posting order:** first the story, then the connected educational content. Test release windows by audience analytics; do not assume one best time for all global markets.

**Speaking to camera:** at least ~1 out of 4 narrative posts features the hero deliberately turning to the viewer and speaking one or two short English sentences. His face remains **mouthless**. The camera connection is conveyed by eye direction, shot size, posture and voiceover or subtitles added in post-production—not by inventing realistic lips.

**Narration tone:** intimate and lightly funny, honest without melodrama. Maximum one key emotional sentence per story. Example lines: “I showed up.” / “Not every day is a personal best.” / “I can miss someone and still move forward.”

**CTA discipline:** mainly *save / learn / try an accessible version / come back tomorrow*; avoid paid-app pitches in the first half. App waitlist is only a later option after real audience validation.

## 8. Production pipeline / automation contract

1. **Select episode by ID** from this immutable narrative plan; attach summary of the previous episode and current strength stage.
2. **Write a short beat sheet** before generating any image; list per-frame camera, action, characters, emotion, location, costume and continuity objects.
3. **Lock character seed/reference sheet** from `BLOB_CHARACTER_VISUAL_BIBLE.md` and source character frames. Treat those images as identity references rather than generating a new mascot.
4. **Generate text-free base frames** with one clear action each; square 1:1. Track filenames `EP001_FRAME01.png` etc.
5. **QA**: mascot silhouette, proportions, eye count, outfit, scene geography, weight progression, non-human texture, camera gaze, realistic exercise cues, any unwanted text or stray limbs. Reject and regenerate any inconsistent frame.
6. **Add English typography / subtitle / VO** separately after image approval. Educational claim review should be separate from creative review.
7. **Export** IG carousel (square), optional 9:16 vertical edit using safe reframing, and reusable app/pose assets; don't fake image dimensions by stretching.
8. **Publish and learn** from save-rate, completion, share-rate, profile taps, follows and eventually lead signups; preserve the spine of the story while iterating execution.
9. **Archive assets + metadata**: episode ID, day, character stage, pose, scene, hook, CTA, prompt version, QA flags and outcomes.

## 9. Fitness and story credibility gates

- Exercises shown must have safe, credible movement cues; a coach/qualified reviewer should approve instructional publishing. AI-generated anatomy or two-position diagrams alone are not proof of correct form.
- Never imply that training a body part alone burns fat there; never show instant muscle growth, miracle cures, or a precise transformation guarantee.
- Keep rest days, light days and adaptation in the story. Avoid exercising hard every day or working through sharp pain, illness, or serious discomfort.
- The protagonist's emotional health matters. Fitness offers structure and agency, not a cure for betrayal, rejection or distress.
- No invented medical credentials, endorsements or performance statistics. Any app launch/monetization claims require their own validation.

## 10. Serialized cliffhanger rhythm

- Every narrative episode ends with either **a small victory**, **a relatable setback**, or **one clear next-step question**.
- At least once every 7 days, show the protagonist **addressing the camera**, reflecting on what changed emotionally, not just physically.
- Every week shows at least one rest/recovery beat, one technical skill, one social moment and one quiet private moment.
- Camera, color, environments and side characters should evolve gently, while the protagonist remains visually immediately identifiable.

**Core line:** *He didn't become a different person. He learned to show up as himself.*
